LOLM’s product claim is persistent task-state control — not “another smarter model.” A structured latent model of the task is maintained and used to choose the next action.

Latent task state · zt

After every observation, action, and result, LOLM updates:

z_t = { G, P, W, A, U, F, C }

  • G — goals and priority
  • P — plan / dependency steps
  • W — world / workspace (files, exits, browser)
  • A — assumptions and commitments
  • U — uncertainty and open questions
  • F — failures, contradictions, dead ends
  • C — completion criteria + evidence

Policy: at+1 = π(zt+1) — continue, retrieve, verify, branch, or finalize. Finalize is refused when completion criteria still lack evidence. Producing output ≠ task complete.

Honest scope: We do not claim LOLM currently beats Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or Devin on every multi-hour software job or every public coding benchmark. Those head-to-heads are not published here. We claim the architecture and what we measure on our harness and receipts.

Infrastructure that serves zt

NFET (five-stream control: surface · latent SSM · regime · gate · memory) measures entropy, drift, and related signals and can force verify, retrieve, branch, or finalize. Coding and visual loops use the same control vocabulary. Multi-model race and free cascade are how LOLM gets candidates — they are not the category claim.

What we deliberately hide

Lab dashboards live under More in the workspace. The product loop: describe work → hold the plot → run in a jail → verify when required → seal a receipt.
